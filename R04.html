The Education Hill Housing Stock

Most Education Hill homes went up between the late seventies and the mid-nineties, which puts their plumbing in a specific generation. Supply lines are typically copper — good news, since copper lasts decades when water chemistry is kind, and Redmond's water is relatively gentle on it. Drain lines are usually ABS plastic, which holds up well but can develop belly sags where soil settled under slab sections. Foundations are a mix of slab-on-grade and crawlspace, and that matters: crawlspace homes give us access to run new lines or find leaks, while slab homes mean any under-floor issue is a bigger conversation. Original water heaters are long gone — if yours is the second or third unit, check its age, because the 10-to-12-year lifespan is the single most predictable failure in this neighborhood. Knowing your home's era tells us where to look first, which is why we always ask the build year when you call.

The Usual Suspects We Get Called For

Water heaters lead the Education Hill call log by a wide margin. A 12-year-old tank doesn't fail gracefully — it fails on a Monday morning with forty gallons on the garage floor. We replace a lot of them here, and we always check the anode rod math with you: maintain it and the next one lasts longer. Running toilets are next — worn flappers and fill valves that hike water bills silently for months. Garbage disposals jammed by ambitious family cooking (potato peels and Thanksgiving week are our busy season), hose bibbs split by winter freezes, and the slow bathroom drain that three kids' worth of hair finally defeated. None of these are exotic. They're the predictable wear items of busy family homes, and most are fixed in a single visit with parts we carry on the truck.

Family-Proofing Your Plumbing

Kids are harder on plumbing than time is, so we think in terms of durability. Upgrade toilet fill valves to quality brass-shank models instead of the cheapest plastic — they survive little hands jiggling handles. Teach the household what the disposal can and can't take (our rule: if you wouldn't chew it comfortably, the disposal shouldn't either). Tag your main water shutoff with a bright label and show every family member old enough to reach it — in a burst-pipe emergency, the ten minutes you save finding the shutoff is the difference between a mop-up and a remodel. Consider a water heater maintenance visit before year ten: flushing sediment and checking the anode rod can add years to the tank's life. And if your home still has its original hose bibbs, swap them for frost-free models before the next cold snap. Small, cheap, and exactly the kind of prevention that keeps our emergency line quiet.

When to Call vs. Weekend Project

We're honest about this: not everything needs a plumber. Safe weekend projects include replacing a toilet flapper, cleaning an aerator, hitting the disposal's reset button, or swapping a showerhead. Call us for anything behind a wall, anything involving gas, sewer odors, water where it shouldn't be, no hot water, or an active leak you can't stop. The gray zone — a slow drain, a faucet that drips, a toilet that runs — is where judgment matters: a $12 flapper is DIY, but a toilet that runs after a new flapper has a deeper problem. Our rule of thumb for Education Hill homeowners: if you've tried the obvious fix once and it didn't work, stop before you break something. A service call is always cheaper than a service call plus the damage from the attempt.
